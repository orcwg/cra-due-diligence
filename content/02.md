# 2.1 To what do we apply due diligence

Under the [EU Cyber Resilience Act][CRA] (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847), manufacturers integrating open source software
into commercial offerings assume direct legal responsibility for the security posture of those third-party components.
[Article 13(5)][article-13] explicitly mandates that manufacturers exercise due diligence when incorporating third-party
elements into Products with Digital Elements.

This chapter clarifies in which cases should a manufacturer execute an [article 13(5)][article-13] due diligence. 

## Which products

As it is stated in the [introduction](index.md#introduction) due diligence is needed only for products placed to the
market of the European Union. The performance of [article 13(5)][article-13] due diligence is needed only if the product
was placed in the European Union market after 11 December 2027 or if it was placed in the market before the 11 December
2027 date, but it had substantial modifications after. 

Due diligence should be performed during the development of all products and their associated remote data processing
solutions that are part of the product offering.
This includes not only the software and hardware products, but also all remotely operated services that are required for
the intended use of the product.

The CRA distinguishes between purely non-commercial upstream open source development and commercial product
distribution. Due diligence obligations apply strictly to commercial entities placing Products with Digital Elements
(PDEs) on the EU market.

Primary Target Categories

- **Commercial Hardware & Connected Devices**: Industrial controllers, Internet of Things (IoT) hardware, network
  equipment, and consumer electronics that integrate open source firmware or software stacks.
- **Commercial Software Products**: Standalone enterprise software, edge-connected applications, desktop and mobile
  applications, and embedded operating systems distributed within the EU.
- **Monetised Open Source Offerings**: Open source projects distributed in the context of a commercial activity
  (e.g., bundled with paid technical support, enterprise licenses, or premium features).

Regulatory Boundary: [Recital 27][recital-27] of the CRA clarifies that non-commercial open source developers and
foundations are exempt from direct manufacturing obligations. However, the moment a commercial manufacturer integrates
a non-commercial open source component into its product, that component falls fully within the manufacturer's regulatory
compliance scope.

## Which components of the product

[Article 13(5)][article-13] due diligence should be performed for all third party software and hardware components
integrated to the product. While the requirement applies equally to commercial, proprietary, free, and open source
components, this whitepaper focuses exclusively on open source software components.

Due diligence cannot be limited to top-level dependencies. Regulatory compliance requires complete transparency across
the entire software supply chain, encompassing all open source code compiled, linked, or executed within the final
product.

Target Component Hierarchy:

- **Direct & Transitive Dependencies**: Primary open source libraries and frameworks imported directly by developers, as
  well as all indirect (nested/transitive) sub-dependencies down the dependency graph.
- **System & Runtime Infrastructure**: Embedded operating systems (e.g., Linux), container base images, hypervisors, and
  execution runtimes when they are included the product.

!!! note
    In typical cloud cases the hypervisors, container runtime environments or other parts of the cloud infrastrucutre
    are not part of the product.

- **Firmware & Hardware Abstraction Layers**: Open source bootloaders, drivers, and microcode integrated into hardware
  platforms when they are included in the product.
- **Bundled Runtime Utilities**: External scripts, third-party binary blobs, or open source utility engines embedded to
  deliver core product functionality

```markdown
├── Application Layer        └─ Direct Dependencies (Frameworks, Libraries)
├── Middleware Layer         └─ Transitive Sub-Dependencies (Nested Packages)
├── Runtime / Execution      └─ Containers, Virtual Machines, Language Runtimes
└── System Layer             └─ Embedded OS (e.g., Linux), Firmware, Microcode
```

## When to apply [article 13(5)][article-13] due diligence?

The [article 13(5)][article-13] due diligence should be performed before or at the time a third party component is
integrated into a product. 
When a new product release or an update containing substantial modifications (see [article 3(30)][article-3]) is
prepared for placement on the market, the due diligence assessment shall be repeated for all third-party components
integrated into the product.
[Article 13(5)][article-13] due diligence applies as part of the product development processes used to evaluate and
integrate third party components and is not limited to any particular organizational team or function.
